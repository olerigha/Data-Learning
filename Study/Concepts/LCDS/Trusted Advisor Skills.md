# Trusted Advisor Skills
**In one line:** Technical skills get you hired; human skills (executive communication, listening, stakeholder management, etiquette) earn trust and keep clients.

**In plain words:** Clients rarely remember who wrote the best Python. They remember who helped them make better decisions. So translate tech into business value, listen for the real problem behind the request, keep every stakeholder on board (a perfect model nobody adopts is a failed project), and be reliable in the small things: on time, prepared, doing what you said.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Executive communication | outcomes, not algorithms ("precision and recall" → "reducing costly mistakes") |
| Active listening / discovery | uncover the root problem behind symptoms |
| Stakeholder map | sponsors, business leaders, SMEs, engineers, IT/security, legal, end users |
| Trusted advisor | retained for trust, not just expertise |
| Business etiquette | small behaviors that reduce friction for others |

**Key points**
- **Executive communication:** simple language, stories with data, recommendations rather than raw analysis; tailor to the audience (method for scientists, implementation for engineers, risk/cost/ROI/timeline for executives).
- **Discovery:** listen more than you speak, ask open-ended questions, clarify assumptions. A chatbot request may really be a knowledge-management problem.
- **Stakeholders:** build trust early, manage expectations continuously, keep everyone informed. Change management starts in the Define stage.
- **Trusted-advisor mindset:** understand the business first, be clear, deliver what you promise, admit what you don't know, keep learning.
- **Etiquette:** respect the context, commitments (own mistakes fast; never let someone discover a problem you knew about), time and attention.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the four critical consulting skills.
