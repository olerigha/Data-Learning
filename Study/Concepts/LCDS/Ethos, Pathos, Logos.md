# Ethos, Pathos, Logos
**In one line:** Three ways to persuade: trust in you (ethos), feelings (pathos), and reasoning (logos).

**In plain words:** People say yes to people they trust and like, so credibility comes first. A good argument also makes sense (logic and evidence) and connects to what the audience cares about (emotion). The lecture puts weight on ethos: trust beats credentials. Listen more than you talk.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Ethos | credibility and character: integrity, knowledge, goodwill, sincerity |
| Pathos | appeal to emotion |
| Logos | appeal to reason: logic and evidence |

**Key points**
- **Elements of ethos:** honesty, integrity, knowledge, goodwill, sincerity.
- **Ethos tactics:** cite sources, avoid extreme positions, take opposing views seriously, organize the argument, don't read your slides, use people's names, never talk down, be yourself.
- "Big ears, big eyes, small mouth": the first rule of speaking is to listen.
- Chapter 7 lists "argue by reason" (logos) and "appeal to emotion" (pathos) as separate steps.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the three appeals and the five elements of ethos.
