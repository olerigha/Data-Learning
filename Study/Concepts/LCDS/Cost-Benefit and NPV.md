# Cost-Benefit and NPV
**In one line:** Make the business case by listing costs and benefits (tangible and intangible), then use Net Present Value to judge whether the money is worth it.

**In plain words:** Executives fund projects that pay back. List what the project costs (build and run), what it returns (revenue, costs avoided, productivity), and softer effects (morale, strategic and competitive impact). Money later is worth less than money now, so NPV shrinks each future year's cash flow by a discount rate and adds them up. Positive NPV means the project creates value.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Project costs | build spending: project expenses + capital expenditures |
| Operating costs / benefits | ongoing: revenues, cost avoidance, productivity savings |
| Intangibles | morale, strategic impact, competitive impact |
| NPV | Net Present Value: today's value of all future cash flows minus the investment |
| Discount rate ($r$) | yearly rate used to shrink future money (e.g. 8%) |
| $C_t$ | net cash flow in year $t$ |

**Key points**
- $\text{NPV}=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\dfrac{C_t}{(1+r)^t}$
  In words: shrink each year's net cash by $(1+r)$ once per year of waiting, then add them all up. Year 0 is usually the negative investment.
- NPV > 0 ⇒ worth doing at that discount rate.
- Rationalize-stage checklist: identify stakeholders, SMEs, the business need, who benefits, and alternatives.

**Example:** Invest $100K now and get $60K a year for 2 years at 8%: $-100 + 60/1.08 + 60/1.08^2 \approx +7$K, so it's worth it.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the NPV idea and "positive = go".
