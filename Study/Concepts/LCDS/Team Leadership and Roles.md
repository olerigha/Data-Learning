# Team Leadership and Roles
**In one line:** A team needs a formal leader (assigned or rotated), process roles (scribe, timekeeper, facilitator…), and informal leadership from everyone.

**In plain words:** Formal leadership can be assigned by the organization or the team, or rotated by task or phase. Leaders diagnose problems, set direction, keep roles clear, motivate people, and manage the group's mood and outside relations. Teams also run better when someone owns each process job: taking notes, watching time, holding the parking lot, playing devil's advocate. And leadership isn't one person's job: any member can move the team toward its goal.

**Terms**
| Role | Does |
|---|---|
| Scribe | notes decisions, actions, owners and deadlines, not every remark |
| Parking lot keeper | tracks important off-topic items for later |
| Timekeeper | flags when an item nears its time limit |
| Charter monitor | checks decisions match the team charter |
| Facilitator | runs the agenda, keeps focus, manages turn-taking ("stacking"), draws out quiet members |
| Quality control / devil's advocate | checks the work / challenges the consensus |
| Informal leadership | influence toward the goal by any member |

**Key points**
- Leaders born or made? Trait view (select for it) vs behavioral view (train it); the slide leaves it open.
- Leader tasks: diagnose problems, generate and implement solutions, set direction, manage operations, develop the team, promote learning, manage external relations, motivate, keep roles clear, manage emotions.
- Data science team roles: analyst, ML engineer, data engineer, statistician, viz specialist, domain expert, product manager, AI ethicist, translator, scrum master, storyteller, change manager.
- "Leadership is a process … performed by many of a team's members" (Levi).

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the process roles and that leadership can be shared.
