# Problem Statements
**In one line:** A clear, specific, scoped statement of the business problem you're solving and why it matters. It's the first deliverable of every project.

**In plain words:** Clients often describe symptoms, not the real problem. Before building anything, dig for the core issue, then write it precisely: what you're trying to achieve, how you'll measure success, what's in and out of scope, and what happens if nothing changes. A good problem statement keeps the whole team aimed at the same target.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Background and context | facts, statistics and prior research around the problem |
| Scope and limitations | what's included, what's excluded, constraints |
| Impact and importance | why it's worth solving; the cost of not solving it |
| Economic levers / barriers / gaps | where value comes from, what blocks it, what's missing |

**Key points**
- Four parts: background → problem statement (precise, not vague) → scope and limitations → impact.
- **Good example:** "Detect potentially fraudulent transactions in real time, reducing fraud by 30% within the first year, without significantly increasing false positives."
  In words: what to build, a measurable target, a deadline, and a guardrail.
- Compare it with "Increase sales by identifying the factors that influence customer behavior" (also on the slide): that one has no target or time frame, so the fraud version is easier to act on and check.
- Five discovery questions: What decision are you trying to improve? How is success measured? What happens if nothing changes? Who will use these insights? What data do you trust today?

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the four parts and the five discovery questions.
