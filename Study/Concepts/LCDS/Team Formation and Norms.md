# Team Formation and Norms
**In one line:** Teams go through predictable stages (Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning), and agreed norms in a team charter help them get through.

**In plain words:** New teams start polite (forming), then clash over roles and approaches (storming). Clarifying responsibilities and agreeing how to work together gets them to norming, then performing. They wrap up and celebrate at the end (adjourning). Norms are the team's working rules: some about the task (deadlines, notes, meeting times), some about how people treat each other (listening, compromising). Writing them in a charter makes issues easier to raise.

**Terms**
| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Forming | orientation, getting acquainted |
| Storming | conflict and competition over roles and approach |
| Norming | consensus, clear roles, trust rebuilt |
| Performing | focused on high performance and problem-solving |
| Adjourning | wrap up and celebrate |
| Task norms | agendas, attendance, deadlines, minutes, roles, promptness |
| Maintenance norms | listening, facilitating, compromising, expressing feelings, having fun |
| Team charter | written norms, time frames, delegation and process checks |

**Key points**
- Why norms: they make issues easier to discuss, express core values, define behavior, make it predictable, and build team identity. They aren't fixed; change them as needed.
- Charter examples: internal deadline before Wednesday night; dry run before class; rotate the weekly note-taker; poll everyone before deciding; "Is this the best use of our time right now?"

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the five stages in order and task vs maintenance norms.
