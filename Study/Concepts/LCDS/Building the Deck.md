# Building the Deck
**In one line:** Plan the story, sketch it, then make slides that each stand on their own for an executive.

**In plain words:** Don't open PowerPoint first. Work out what you want to say (a bullet outline), then how you'll say it (a storyboard: rough sketches of each page), and only then build the slides. Tailor everything to who's in the room and why. For senior executives, every slide must make sense with no one talking over it. Keep a standard structure: agenda, page numbers, sources, appendix.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Storyboard | rough sketch of each page's layout and visuals |
| Context / Story / Details | the three content dimensions: situation, logical flow, supporting modules |
| Breadcrumbs | marker showing where you are in the agenda |
| Appendix | backup detail, still with storyline titles |
| CxO | C-level executive (CEO, CIO…) |

**Key points**
- Process: **Plan** (What do I want to say?) → **Story** (How will I say it?) → **Write** (How will I show it?).
- Tailor to: desired outcome, seniority, power dynamics, audience size, location, occasion, culture, language, duration.
- Structure checklist: title page, agenda (repeat it with breadcrumbs), page numbers, sources on almost every slide ("own analysis" when it's yours), summaries, appendix.
- Story checklist: one theme, natural flow, finite takeaways, varied visuals, concise: "Enough, but no more!"
- Level of detail: neither TEDx-light nor extreme detail suits a CxO.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize Plan → Story → Write and "every slide stands on its own".
