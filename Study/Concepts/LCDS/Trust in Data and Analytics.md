# Trust in Data and Analytics
**In one line:** Analytics only creates value if people trust the data, trust how customer data is handled, and work in a culture that encourages asking questions (MIT SMR Connections report, 2019).

**In plain words:** Like a clean lab or a clear chain of evidence, data needs visible care before people believe the insights built on it. The report finds three areas of trust. Trust in the data itself comes from governance, grading data quality and partnering with domain experts. Trust from customers comes from security and privacy, which can be a competitive advantage. Trust in innovation comes from a culture of data literacy, collaboration and experimentation.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Data governance | rules and roles for managing data as an asset |
| Data literacy | the workforce's ability to read and question data |
| Centralized vs decentralized analytics | one central team vs analysts embedded in business units |
| Analytics maturity | how advanced an organization's use of analytics is |

**Key points**
- **Section I, trust in data:** trust advances analytics maturity; grade your data; partner with domain experts; build AI on governance; treat data as an asset.
- **Section II, customer trust:** security and privacy are pivotal; privacy is an opportunity; trust is fragile.
- **Section III, culture:** drive data literacy, foster collaboration, choose centralize vs decentralize deliberately, communicate and educate.
- Cases: Cleveland Clinic (central data store), Caterpillar (strategy per business unit), DataSF (teaching analytical questions), Barclays US (cross-functional teams).

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the three kinds of trust.
