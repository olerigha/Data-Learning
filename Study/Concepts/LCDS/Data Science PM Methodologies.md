# Data Science PM Methodologies
**In one line:** A controlled experiment (Saltz et al., 2017) compared four ways to run data science projects; Kanban worked best and, surprisingly, Scrum worked worst.

**In plain words:** Most research is about data science *techniques*, not how teams should *work together*. Teams ran the same project using one of four approaches: Kanban, Scrum, CRISP-DM, or no defined process (the baseline). Teams using Kanban did best and Scrum teams did worst. One plausible reading: fixed-length sprints fit uncertain, exploratory data work less well than a continuous flow.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Kanban | Agile method: visual board of tasks, limits on work-in-progress, continuous flow (no fixed sprints) |
| Scrum | Agile method with time-boxed sprints (see [[Scrum]]) |
| CRISP-DM | Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining: business understanding → data understanding → preparation → modeling → evaluation → deployment |
| Baseline / ad hoc | no defined process |

**Key points**
- The paper's evaluation model has four factors: **project results**, **ease of use**, **team's willingness to work together again**, **individual satisfaction**.
- Finding: methodology made a significant difference. **Kanban was most effective; Scrum least.**
- Takeaway: pick a process that fits the uncertainty of data science work; don't assume Scrum by default.
