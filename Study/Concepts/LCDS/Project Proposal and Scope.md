# Project Proposal and Scope
**In one line:** Before work starts, agree in writing on the problem, what will be delivered, how success is judged, the time frame, who commits what, and what's in and out of scope.

**In plain words:** Projects go wrong when the client and the consultants picture different results. A proposal pins it down. It restates the client's situation ("Our Understanding"), sets out the objectives, defines scope (and explicitly what's *out*), describes the step-by-step approach with deliverables and a week-by-week schedule, shows the team structure, and lists how much time the client's people must give. Signing it means everyone agreed.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Problem statement | the agreed issue to solve |
| Acceptance criteria | deliverables plus quality / performance metrics that define "done" |
| Our Understanding | consultant's restatement of the client's situation and needs |
| In / out of scope | explicitly included / excluded work |
| Steering committee | senior group that oversees the engagement |
| Statement of Work (SOW) | the formal agreement the schedule depends on |
| PoC | proof of concept |

**Key points**
- A project definition needs: problem statement, acceptance criteria, time constraint, resources (client and team commitments, availability, responsiveness, money).
- Proposal anatomy: Our Understanding & Objectives → Scope → Process → Approach with deliverables → detailed schedule (workshops, status meetings) → team org chart → client time commitments.
- Example: a 10-week analytics roadmap engagement with 3 workshops, weekly status and a final readout. In scope: vision, strategy, governance, roles, information life cycle. Out of scope: metrics, infrastructure.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the four parts of a project definition and why out-of-scope matters.
