# SMART Goals
**In one line:** Good goals are Measurable (a number) and Time-based (a deadline). Those two letters of SMART do most of the work.

**In plain words:** "Improve my visualization skills" isn't a goal you can check. "Take two online courses, 4 hours a week, over the next 2 months" is. SMART stands for Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-based, but the instructor's advice is to focus on the M (a KPI) and the T (a deadline). Also review last period's goals and compare yours with your team's.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| SMART | Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-based |
| Measurable | has a KPI: a number, % change, or other checkable result |
| Time-based | has an end date: "end of Q1", "in 6 weeks" |
| KPI | Key Performance Indicator |

**Key points**
- "Reduce" or "improve" isn't measurable unless a number follows it.
- Examples: "30% less data-cleaning time per project by end of Q1"; "model accuracy from 85% to 90% by end of March".
- Make goal-setting an organizational process: look back at previous goals (what slipped, and why), and align your goals with others'.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize "focus on M and T" and test every goal for both.
