# Elements of Persuasive Argument
**In one line:** Five steps to persuade: focus on your goal, tailor to the decision-maker, build a strong argument, use delivery, and engage the audience.

**In plain words:** Know exactly what you want them to decide. Shape the case around the person deciding: what they already believe, how strongly, and how they like to think. Arrange the argument with a strong opening, at most three main points, and a clear ask at the end. Back it with evidence and examples, and earn trust. Then deliver it well and involve the audience rather than lecturing.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Decision-maker profile | their attitude (direction, intensity, salience) and personality type |
| Direction / intensity / salience | for or against / how strongly / how important it is to them |
| Personality types | Analytic, Driver, Amiable, Emotive |
| Call to action | the specific thing you ask them to do |
| Concede weak points | admit the gaps before someone else raises them |

**Key points**
1. **Focus on your goal:** clarify the main goal plus secondary ones; avoid tangents; prepare for questions.
2. **Tailor to the decision-maker:** people stay consistent with what they've said before, so build on their past statements.
3. **Build a strong argument:**
   - *Introduction:* strong first impression (a startling fact, a question, a story).
   - *Body:* a structure such as problem → solution, or comparing alternatives; at most 3 points; repeat key points.
   - *Conclusion:* ask for the specific action and close with conviction.
   - Use industry examples, a clear "so what", and credibility (ethos).
4. **Use delivery:** language, pace, gestures, eye contact, time.
5. **Engage the audience:** invite participation, seek feedback, ask rhetorical questions, avoid demands and pleas.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the five steps and the intro / body / conclusion moves.
