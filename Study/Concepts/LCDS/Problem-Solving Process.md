# Problem-Solving Process
**In one line:** Three stages: define the problem, generate and select solutions, then implement and evaluate. Teams most often skip the first.

**In plain words:** Teams love to jump to solutions before they understand the problem, hand off the solution to others who weren't involved, and never check whether it worked. Instead, first separate symptoms from root causes and agree on the gap between now and where you want to be. Then brainstorm widely before judging, and pick using agreed criteria. Finally, plan the rollout with owners and milestones, keep the recommenders involved, and measure the result against the original problem.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Five whys | keep asking "why?" to get from symptom to root cause |
| Root cause analysis | finding the underlying cause |
| Process mapping | draw the current and desired state |
| Force field analysis | driving forces vs restraining forces; reducing the restraints works best |
| Criteria matrix | weighted or unweighted table for rating options |
| Diverge / converge | generate many options / narrow to one |

**Key points**
1. **Recognize and define:** gather symptoms, find causes, agree on the current vs desired gap.
2. **Generate and select:** diverge first, then converge with a criteria matrix; pick what the team can commit to.
3. **Implement and evaluate:** action plan with owners and milestones; evaluate and feed lessons back (restarts the OODA loop).
- Why teams struggle: reusing old solutions, emotional rather than logical thinking, limited time/money/information, settling for "good enough", communication issues, politics.
- "Defining and evaluating the problem is the most difficult step for teams" (Levi).

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the three stages and two techniques (five whys, criteria matrix).
