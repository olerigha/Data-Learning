# After-Action Review
**In one line:** After a project, the team asks what to keep doing, stop doing and start doing, and carries those lessons into the next one.

**In plain words:** Right after the McDonald's presentation, each team looked back before starting Verizon. Ask three questions across every part of the work: problem, timing, roles, slides, presenting, leadership. Write the answers up in a page or two. The case debrief adds the analytical lessons: how to measure lift properly and what data traps to watch for.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| AAR | After-Action Review |
| Keep / Stop / Start | what went well / what caused issues / what to try |
| Lift | the extra effect caused by a change (e.g. a promotion), compared with a baseline |
| Cannibalization | a new offer stealing sales from your own existing products |

**Key points**
- **Review areas:** problem understanding and answer; timing and scheduling; work assignments and roles; slide writing and QA; presenting (enough practice?); leadership and coordination.
- **McDonald's outcome:** a restaurant-level time-series model showed All-Day Breakfast interrupted the transaction decline without cannibalizing lunch, so the full breakfast menu stayed all-day until COVID.
- **Analysis lessons:** measure lift (is it significant?), don't mix aggregate and item-level data, handle missing data and stores (new / closed), outliers, seasonality, compliance (did all stores run it the same way?), advertising, price changes, profit by item.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize Keep / Stop / Start and the review areas.
