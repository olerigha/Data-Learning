# Meeting Management
**In one line:** Decide whether you need a meeting at all, then plan it, run it to time, and follow through on decisions.

**In plain words:** Many meetings shouldn't exist. Only meet when there's a decision to make, a problem to solve, or a relationship to build; otherwise send a note. If you do meet, match the format to the purpose, send an agenda and pre-reads ahead of time, keep each item to its time, hear from everyone, and end with clear decisions, owners and deadlines. Send minutes within 24 hours.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Timebox | fixed time slot for an agenda item |
| Parking lot | list for important but off-topic items |
| Pre-read | material sent ahead so the meeting can be about deciding |
| Minutes | record of decisions and action items (not a transcript) |
| AAR | After Action Report: review for high-stakes meetings |

**Key points**
- **Meet when:** a decision needs several people, the topic is sensitive or ambiguous, alignment or trust is needed, or the work is creative. **Don't** for broadcasts or status updates.
- **Formats:** decision (30 min, owner + 3–5), working session (60–90 min, 3–6 doers), status review (15–25 min), brainstorm (45 min, 5–8 diverse people). Pick the shortest that works.
- **Before:** one-sentence purpose, agenda with times, only the needed people, pre-reads 24h ahead, roles assigned.
- **During:** open with purpose, hold timeboxes, draw out quiet voices, park tangents, close with a recap.
- **After:** minutes within 24h (decisions first), share with absentees, track actions, archive, gather feedback.
- Pitfalls → fixes: no purpose → cancel; wrong people → invite by role; drift → timekeeper + parking lot; loud few → round-robin; no decisions → 2-minute recap.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the "meet only if" rule and the before / during / after checklist.
