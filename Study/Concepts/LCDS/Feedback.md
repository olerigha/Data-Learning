# Feedback
**In one line:** Give feedback about specific behavior and its impact, then what to do next; receive it by saying thank you first and deciding later.

**In plain words:** Good feedback describes what someone *did* (words, tone, body language, work product), not their attitude or intentions, then explains the effect and suggests what to do in future. Give it often, keep it short, and give more positive than negative so the negative is easier to hear. Receiving is the other half: feedback bounces off when it triggers us, so separate accepting it from agreeing with it. To get better input, ask for **advice** rather than feedback.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Behavior-focused | what was observable, not "bad attitude" |
| Manager Tools model | ask permission → describe behavior → describe impact → what to do in future |
| Truth / relationship / identity triggers | "that's wrong" / "not from you" / "I'm a failure" (Stone & Heen) |
| Advice vs feedback | advice invites concrete future suggestions; feedback invites judging the past |

**Key points**
- Five keys: behavior-focused, frequent, brief, well-delivered, balanced.
- "You rolled your eyes, sat back and crossed your arms" ✔ vs "You had a bad attitude" ✘.
- The "future" step doesn't always need a request.
- Receiving: say thank you, ask "What did I do or say?", decide later.
- Harvard research (Yoon et al.): asking for advice produced more actionable suggestions.
- Team peer rubric: communication, collaboration, contribution to goals, accountability (scored 1–4).

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize behavior → impact → future, and "ask for advice".
