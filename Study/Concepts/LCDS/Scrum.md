# Scrum
**In one line:** An Agile method where a small, self-organizing team works through a prioritized backlog in 2–4 week sprints, with a daily standup and review and retrospective meetings.

**In plain words:** The Product Owner keeps a ranked to-do list of features (the product backlog), written as user stories. At sprint planning the team pulls the top items into a sprint backlog and commits to a sprint goal. Every day there's a short standup. At the end, the team shows what it built (sprint review), reflects on how it worked (retrospective), and plans the next sprint. The Scrum Master clears blockers and protects the process.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Sprint | fixed 14–28 day work cycle ending in a usable increment |
| Product backlog | all wanted features, ranked by the Product Owner |
| Sprint backlog | items the team commits to this sprint |
| User story | smallest unit of value: "As a ⟨role⟩ I want ⟨what⟩ so that ⟨why⟩" |
| Epic | a group of related user stories |
| Daily standup | 15-minute daily sync |
| Backlog refinement | regularly tidying and estimating the backlog |
| Sprint review / retrospective | demo the work / improve the process |

**Key points**
- **Roles:** *Scrum Master* (servant-leader and coach; removes impediments), *Product Owner* (represents stakeholders; owns and prioritizes the backlog; usually a business expert), *Team members* (everyone else).
- **Team:** 5–10 people, cross-functional (business, data engineering, data science, QA, developers, UI), ideally full-time, self-organizing; membership changes only between sprints.
- **Example story:** "As a risk manager, I'd like the lending app to predict the chance of loan default for an applicant, so I can make an informed lending decision." It belongs to a "Customer Risk Evaluation" epic.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the three roles, the sprint events, and the user-story template.
