# Inductive vs Deductive Reasoning
**In one line:** Inductive goes from specific cases to a general conclusion (bottom-up); deductive goes from general premises to a specific conclusion (top-down).

**In plain words:** Inductive: "These five stores saw sales rise after the promotion, so promotions likely raise sales." It generalizes from examples, so it can be wrong. Deductive: "All contracts over $1M need board approval; this is $2M; so it needs board approval." If the premises are true, the conclusion must be. Both lead to a "therefore"; pick the one that fits your evidence and audience.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Premise | a statement the argument starts from |
| Inductive | specific → general; based on inference, analogy or causal correlation |
| Deductive | general → specific; the conclusion follows if the premises are true |
| Bottom-up / top-down | inductive / deductive |

**Key points**
- Inductive is strong with data: many examples, trends, correlations. Watch out for correlation vs causation.
- Deductive is strong with rules and agreed principles.
- Slide decks often use the "tell them up front" structure: state the conclusion first, then support it (see [[Pyramid Principle]]).

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the direction of each (specific→general vs general→specific).
