# Team Dynamics and Conflict
**In one line:** How a team communicates, builds trust and handles disagreement decides what it delivers. Debate about the work is good; personal conflict is not.

**In plain words:** Forming the team is only the start. Strong teams argue about ideas, methods and interpretations (task conflict), which sharpens the work, but they don't make it personal (relationship conflict), which corrodes trust. Too little conflict is also a problem: people quietly go along with a decision nobody believes in. Surface disagreement early, keep it about the facts, and resolve it before it hardens.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Task conflict / constructive controversy | debating ideas and methods (healthy) |
| Relationship conflict | personal friction (harmful, especially when work is interdependent) |
| Abilene Paradox | everyone agrees to something no one actually wants |
| Defensive avoidance | going along with a dominant member to avoid conflict |
| Personal vs positional power | influence from who you are vs from your title |
| Team reflexivity | stopping to reflect on how the team works and improve it |

**Key points**
- Early stages are polite; conflict rises once real work starts (roles, status, tasks, boundaries).
- No task conflict → unhealthy agreement, routine work, no improvement.
- Ways to create constructive controversy: bring in outside information, focus on issues and facts not people, use "Yes, and…" from improv.
- Team learning: adaptation, resilience and reflexivity; observing the group's process helps.
