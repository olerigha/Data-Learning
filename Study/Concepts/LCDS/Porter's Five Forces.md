# Porter's Five Forces
**In one line:** Five pressures that decide how profitable an industry is. It's an external view of a company's strategic position.

**In plain words:** Michael Porter (Harvard) argued that profit isn't just about your rivals. Suppliers can squeeze you, customers can push prices down, other products can replace yours, and newcomers can enter. The stronger these five forces, the harder it is to make money in that industry.

**Terms**
| Force | Means |
|---|---|
| Rivalry among firms | how fiercely existing competitors fight |
| Power of suppliers | can suppliers raise prices or cut quality? |
| Power of buyers | can customers force prices down? |
| Threat of substitutes | other products that meet the same need |
| Barriers to entry | how hard it is for new competitors to get in (low barriers = big threat) |

**Key points**
- Use it to understand the industry *before* proposing data science work.
- Five Forces looks outside (industry); the value chain looks inside (company); SWOT covers both.

**Example:** Ride-hailing: many substitutes (taxis, transit), low switching cost for buyers, and drivers who work for several apps all squeeze profit.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize the five forces.
