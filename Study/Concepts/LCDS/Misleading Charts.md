# Misleading Charts
**In one line:** Common chart mistakes that confuse or mislead an audience, even by accident.

**In plain words:** "Lies, damned lies, and statistics" (Disraeli). A chart can be technically correct but still give the wrong impression. Bars that don't start at zero make small gaps look huge. Pies that add up to more than 100% are nonsense. 3D effects and rainbow colors distort what people see. Your credibility depends on visuals being honest and easy to read.

**Terms**
| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Truncated axis | y-axis not starting at 0, which exaggerates differences |
| Axis break | visual mark showing the axis was cut on purpose |
| Visual clutter | too much on one chart (3D, heavy patterns) |
| Counts vs percentages | raw numbers vs shares; picking the wrong one misleads |

**Key points**
- **Start bars, areas and bubbles at zero.** Lines may be cut if small changes matter (e.g. stocks), but show an axis break.
- Typical issues: wrong chart type (pies), variety for its own sake, clutter (3D), color abuse (don't rely on color alone; think color-blind viewers), noisy fills and lines, inconsistent order or scales, counts where percentages are needed, repeated words.
- Student before/after examples: replace text-heavy slides with one clear visual and a storyline title.

**Memorize vs. look up:** Memorize "start at zero" and the issues list.
